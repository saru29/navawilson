**Fault Terminations**

1.  Can be done prior to the end of the lease
2.  The reason must be one listed under the Act, specifically sections 59-67.

**No Fault Terminations**

1.  Can only be given at the end of the term
2.  Should be given in good faith

<table><tbody><tr><td><b>Reason for Termination</b></td><td><b>Form</b></td><td><b>Number of days for Notice</b></td><td><b>Remember:&nbsp;</b></td></tr><tr><td>Personal Use by Landlord or immediate family member or purchaser</td><td>N12</td><td>At least 60 days, cannot be prior to the last day of the tenancy</td><td>The person who requires the unit must reside in the unit for at least a year and the landlord must provide one month’s rent as compensation</td></tr><tr><td>Demolition</td><td>N13</td><td>At least 120 days, cannot be prior to the last day of the tenancy</td><td>The Landlord must give their tenant 3 months rent as compensation or offer a suitable rental unit</td></tr><tr><td>Extensive Repair</td><td>N13</td><td>At least 120 days, cannot be prior to the last day of the tenancy</td><td>If the tenant is not going to move back in after construction, the landlord must give them 3 months rent as compensation.&nbsp;If they are, they must give them 3 months rent as compensation or compensation for the amount of time the unit was under construction; whichever is less</td></tr><tr><td>Converting the Unit</td><td>N13</td><td>At least 120 days, cannot be prior to the last day of the tenancy</td><td>The Landlord must give their tenant 3 months rent as compensation or offer a suitable rental unit</td></tr></tbody></table>

**Good Faith Requirement:**

When providing these any of these notices, the landlord must do so in good faith. It is deemed that the notice is given in _good faith_ and the onus is on the tenant to prove otherwise. The test of good faith is a genuine intention to occupy.
